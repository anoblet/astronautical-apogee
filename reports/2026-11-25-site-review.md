# Andrew Noblet website review

**November 25, 2026 · Site revision `2af642a`**

The site has a coherent editorial style, a human introduction, and a clear way to start a conversation. The highest-impact changes are to fix three reproducible usability defects, serve the designed 404 page in production, and make it easier for prospective clients to find a relevant service and see its price on a phone.

## Priority 2 · Clarify service discovery, pricing, and booking

- **Home and services.** The portrait and short geographic description make the introduction personal. “Good ideas. Thoughtfully built.” and “A clearer presence. A practical next step.” rely on paragraphs to explain the actual offer. Put “Website design, development, and ongoing support” or an equivalent service cue next to the home headline. Give the service rows direct labels and a short outcome. The home overview has no tutoring row even though the service index, menu, and paid tutoring page include it. Add a compact path if tutoring is intended to attract home-page traffic. Remove repeated numbering where the visual order already does the work.
- **Navigation and route hierarchy.** The main menu routes to `/services/hosting/` and `/services/consultation/`, while `/hosting/` and `/consultation/` are separate indexed introductions with related offers. The shorter pages answer concrete problems more directly and omit a direct link to paid plans. Give each a clear role and a path to its detailed service and pricing pages. At `/services/design/pricing/`, the expanded header menu has zero current links. Add a section indicator or breadcrumb for deep service and portfolio routes, with `aria-current="page"` reserved for an exact destination. Keep the working split link/disclosure behavior and Escape focus restoration.
- **Pricing on phones.** At 390×844, the five-card “Explore pricing by service” control precedes the offers on every pricing page. On general pricing the first plan starts around y=869px. On social pricing it starts around y=901px. The design plan starts around y=685px, with its button below the fold. Use a compact service switcher or start with a short package-and-price summary. Add a top-level route to tutoring’s $100 one-hour rate. Keep the useful distinction between website implementation and design-only work near the relevant cards.
- **Package comparison.** Website and design packages use broad labels such as “Basic SEO,” “email support,” and “priority support.” Hosting lists “advanced security” and “enhanced performance.” Translate these into a few comparable inclusions and explain key boundaries where the offering is confirmed, such as page count, handoff, support window, backups and restoration, and separately quoted work. The social pricing page gives a better model with post counts, approval, and reply monitoring. Show the combined monthly cost alongside the hosting bundle discount so the buyer can evaluate it quickly.
- **Inquiry handoff.** “Get Started,” “Get Design,” and “Get Hosting” all lead to the free-call contact flow. The design and tutoring checks reached Contact with the right context, but the visitor is asked to repeat it when booking or emailing. Say “Discuss this package” or “Book a free discovery call” before the click. Present the selected service by both contact options and carry it into the email subject or booking notes where supported. Separate the paid $99 quick consult from the free call in the CTA itself so visitors can choose deliberately. The calendar link resolved to a Google scheduling URL with HTTP 200. Appointment availability and final submission were outside the review.
- **Contact and support.** “Tell me what you have in mind” works as inviting copy. A task label like “Contact Andrew” or “Book a free 30-minute call” would help users orient themselves when they arrive from a package, search, or the footer. Keep the email alternative near the booking button and preserve the practical preparation list. Make the footer’s “Theme” link match “Accessibility settings” and give the legacy `/theme/` route a direct redirect if it only forwards visitors there.

Reviewed all 50 generated HTML pages using Playwright Chromium, including the 16 blog articles, four professional case studies, all service and pricing pages, both short service introductions, the demo and utility routes, the empty author archive, and the generated 404 document.
- Captured and inspected desktop (1440×960) and mobile (390×844) views in light and dark, plus a 320px dark-theme overflow pass across all routes. All 250 default-theme or light-preset page/view combinations loaded locally without page exceptions, document-wide horizontal overflow, or failed images among completed image loads. These checks do not detect hidden content or visual contrast defects, which were checked separately.
- Exercised desktop and mobile navigation, keyboard dismissal, JavaScript-disabled navigation, theme persistence and reset, the large heading preference, tutoring and mentorship anchors, selected inquiry context, and Angular hydration. All 11 existing Playwright checks passed across two runs. The first run ended after eight passing checks, and a targeted run completed the last three.
- Used the local production build for the full crawl and spot-checked deployed tutoring, Lit, Angular, the missing-page response, and the outbound scheduling URL. Cross-browser behavior, assistive technology, a full contrast audit, and third-party booking completion were outside scope.
